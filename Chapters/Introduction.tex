\chapter{INTRODUCTION (Jakub Kinder)}
\label{chap:Introduction}

% Introducing idea of PC building and its versitality
In recent years there has been a significant surge in interest in assembling custom PCs. 
While initially popularized by the video game development industry, whose products demand an increasingly sophisticated and powerful hardware, more people began to notice vast benefits of custom-built computers.
With proper knowledge and skills, one can tailor a computer to precisely fit their needs regarding performance or aesthetics and at the same time, reduce overall cost of the machine, by prioritizing only certain components.
For instance, a professional graphic designer may require a device for Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) intensive tasks such as rendering images and doesn't have much use for full capabilities of the newest Central Processing Unit (CPU).
However, PC building, while simple, requires rather comprehensive knowledge, not precisely of inner workings of a computer, but rather the parts themselves.
It is incredibly crucial to know various types of ports, their versions and how to connect them properly so as not to damage them or components that make up a PC.
Unfortunately, this growth of popularity has not been matched by a corresponding promotion of the necessary knowledge, skills or even adequate means to gain them.
This knowledge gap has led many people to abandon the idea of building their own PC, because not only must the components chosen be compatible with each other, but also they tend to be expensive and relatively easy to damage, due to lack of practical information.

% Introduction to tools and simulators that were developed
In response to this lack of practical resources on the topic, countless tools have been developed with the goal of helping people design or build their own custom PCs.
Most of them, however, focus on the subject of component selection, especially compatibility regarding standards of ports, without taking into account their physical size.
Nonetheless, they have still proven useful for beginners by reducing knowledge required in order to at least choose adequate hardware.
Apart from these basic configurators, there is a niche category of tools that promotes a more in-depth approach to spreading the knowledge on PC assembly.
Such tools took on a form of simulation games, from which the most well known is PC Building Simulator \cite{pcbuilder}, the first installment of entire series of such games.
They differ from previously mentioned tools by providing not only knowledge of proper component selection, but also how assembly itself looks like in 3D environment.
However, these tools are still a simplification of reality; while they may offer glimpses into the process of building a PC or reduce knowledge needed to select components, but they still fail to provide the necessary practical skills.

% Introduction to vr technology and cave
There is one possible and very promising solution to this particular dilemma, though currently unexplored in this context, it is Virtual Reality (VR).
This technology has made strides in its development over the years, especially when it comes to games of various genres, which includes highly interactive simulations.
Moreover, VR is extremely immersive, and most actions performed within its environment are intuitive or demand only minimal adjustments from users.
These characteristics alone make it more than a prime choice of environment for development of new tools for both experienced and novice PC builders.
However, there is an environment that is capable of far superior immersion and interactivity than classic VR, it is called CAVE Automatic Virtual Environment (CAVE).
This technology, surpasses regular VR headset in almost every aspect, instead of displaying the digital world from user's eye level, user's entire body is placed inside the virtual space.
These circumstances bolster immersion by heightening the user's awareness of all their movements, thus allowing for the acquisition of hands-on experience and complex procedural skills within given simulation.

% Introduction to the goal of the project
The goal of this project is to take advantage of aforementioned unexplored environment as well as lack of comprehensive tools to learn about PC building, and develop an extensive PC building simulator in VR environment, specifically CAVE.
Such a solution will demonstrate in depth how to assemble a PC and how the whole process looks like, also it will promote more practical skills to a certain degree.
Besides its educational capabilities, the developed application will give them the opportunity to test various component configurations, an aspect which both novice and experienced users will benefit from.
To validate the system, a short comparative study will be performed on a small group of testers with various levels of experience in PC assembly, which will include surveys and sessions in developed CAVE simulator as well as existing classic 3D solution.
